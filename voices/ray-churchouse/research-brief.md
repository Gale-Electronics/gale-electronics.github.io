# Ray Churchouse — research brief

Working file for searching with ChatGPT (or any search assistant). It is excluded from the built site in `_config.yml`, so it stays in the repo but is not published.

The archive page is `voices/ray-churchouse/index.md`. Findings go into its **Leads to check** section, marked *unverified* until the original source has been seen.

## Prompt to paste into ChatGPT

```text
I am researching Ray Churchouse (also spelt "Churchhouse"), a London hi-fi
salesman and retailer in the 1970s and 1980s, for a historical archive about
Gale Electronics & Design Ltd (Bruton Place, Mayfair; makers of the GS401
loudspeaker and GT2101 turntable; founder Ira Gale).

What I already know (do not repeat it back as a finding):
- He described himself as Ira Gale's first employee and sales manager, and
  said he set up Gale's UK retailers and sales to US military (PX) stores in
  the UK and Germany.
- A colleague remembers him as Gale's salesman, 1974–77, with an E-Type Jaguar.
- He is linked with the London hi-fi shops Hi-Fi-Fo-Fum (South Kensington)
  and Unilet. One account says he later worked with Quad.
- His son is Mark Churchouse.

Please search for:
1. Any printed mention of Ray Churchouse/Churchhouse: hi-fi magazines
   (Hi-Fi News, Hi-Fi Answers, Gramophone, Hi-Fi for Pleasure, What Hi-Fi?),
   trade directories, adverts, show reports, newspaper archives.
2. The history of Hi-Fi-Fo-Fum and of Unilet: owners, addresses, dates
   opened and closed, and any connection to Ray.
3. Any link between Ray and Quad (Acoustical Manufacturing Co.).
4. Companies House or London Gazette records naming him as a director.
5. Gale's sales to US forces exchanges (e.g. RAF Upper Heyford, Lakenheath,
   Mildenhall, and bases in Germany) in the 1970s.

Rules for your answer:
- For every claim give the exact source: publication title, issue/date, page,
  or a working URL. Quote the relevant sentence.
- Say clearly when you cannot find a source. Do not fill gaps with guesses.
- Mark anything from forums, social media or memory-based sites as
  "secondary" and give the poster's name and date.
- Keep different people with similar names apart; say if a match is uncertain.
```

## Checking what comes back

ChatGPT can invent sources that look real. Before a lead goes on the page as **verified**:

- Open the URL, or find the scan (worldradiohistory.com has many UK hi-fi magazines), and confirm the quoted words are there.
- Record the publication, date and page, not just "ChatGPT".
- If the source can't be found, keep the lead as **unverified** or drop it.
