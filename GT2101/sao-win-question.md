# Dr Sao Win and the GT2101: an open question

Later accounts of the Gale GT2101 credit Dr Sao Win as Ira Gale's college friend and co-designer. The patent and the first-hand recollections gathered so far do not support that credit. There is, however, reported provenance that Sao Win himself owned a GT2101, so the story cannot simply be dismissed as a mistake.

This page sets out what is known in three levels: what is documented, what was claimed later, and what is only a hypothesis. The archive is deliberately keeping the uncertainty in view instead of building a neat story out of incomplete evidence.

  The central question<br>
  Why does Sao Win appear in the 1985 <em>Stereophile</em> history of Gale when he does not appear in the 1974 GT2101 patent or in Nigel Hobden's recollection, yet there is now direct provenance evidence that Sao Win himself owned a GT2101?<br><br>
  This question is unresolved. The archive will not answer it without new primary evidence.

## 1. Documented

These points rest on documents or on first-hand testimony that the archive has recorded and attributed. First-hand testimony is marked as such: it is evidence, not proof.

### Kenneth Freivokh's early design

- Kenneth Freivokh presented his thesis, *A Transcription Turntable Unit*, to the Royal College of Art in March 1972. It describes a belt-driven turntable with a three-pointed acrylic "tricept", a sprung base plate on three springs, and stainless-steel and clear acrylic construction. [Full scan and transcription](/GT2101/early-machines/freivokh-thesis/).
- *Design* magazine featured Freivokh's belt-driven acrylic turntable in August 1973. [Early Machines](/GT2101/early-machines/#magazine-article-speak-up-freivokhs-turntable-and-loudspeaker).
- The three-pointed, star-shaped suspended chassis of his design is the form that later appears in the production GT2101. [From prototype to production](/GT2101/#from-prototype-to-production).

How Ira Gale came to use Freivokh's design is not yet documented. The 1981 D. W. Labs letter says the design was "purchased" from a Royal College of Art student, and Nigel Hobden recalls that Gale bought the design and rights. Freivokh says he received no payment, royalties or recognition. No agreement or contract has been found. See [Ken Freivokh's account](/GT2101/early-machines/#ken-freivokhs-account-september-2026).

### The 1974 patent

Patent family DE2547849A1, "Sound record player deck", priority date 25 October 1974 (UK application GB 46296/74), assignee Gale Electronics & Design Ltd. [Patent record](https://patents.google.com/patent/DE2547849A1/en).

The five named inventors are:

- Ronald David Carter
- Kenneth Freivokh
- Ira Dennis Gale
- Nigel Mark Hobden
- Michael Taylor

Sao Win is not named. The patent describes the three-armed chassis, suspension at three spring points, the triangular platter arrangement, direct drive, a magnetic thrust bearing, and an optical speed-sensing and encoder system.

On the evidence of the patent, there is no documentary basis for describing Sao Win as an inventor or co-patentee of the GT2101.

### DCA and the production engineering

- David Carter, founder of DCA, and Nigel Hobden, then at DCA, are both named inventors on the 1974 patent.
- DCA's own studio publicity photographs of the GT2101 survive. [Design by DCA](/GT2101/design-by-DCA/).
- *First-hand testimony:* Paul Ramsden, of DCA's electronics group, says Ira Gale came to DCA with the turntable's look already established and needing help with the electronics and mechanical engineering, and that most of that work was done at DCA. [Interviews & Provenance](/GT2101/interviews-provenance/#engineering-commentary).
- *First-hand testimony:* Nigel Hobden has told the archive that DCA engineered the GT2101, including the electronics, the control tower and the servo loop. [Nigel Hobden's recollection](/GT2101/research-notes/#nigel-hobdens-recollection-september-2026).
- *First-hand testimony:* Nigel Hobden has also said that he has never heard of Sao Win or Win Laboratories in connection with Gale or the GT2101.

Hobden's recollection is important contradictory testimony from someone closely involved in the production engineering. It is not absolute proof that Sao Win had no involvement: Hobden joined Gale after DCA took on the turntable, and his memory is of events about fifty years ago.

### Gale under D. W. Labs and Donald Wong

- After Gale Electronics & Design Ltd failed, Donald Wong bought the company, which continued as D. W. Labs Limited. A letter on D. W. Labs letterhead with the Gale Electronics logo, dated 28 January 1981, lists Donald Wong among the directors. [The 1981 letter](/GT2101/manuals-literature/#letter-from-d-w-labs-limited-to-huub-bouwmeester-28-january-1981).
- Contemporary trade material associates D. W. Labs with the later Gale products, including the GS402 and the later 301 and 302. *(Scans of this material still need to be added to the archive.)*

### The 1985 Stereophile review

Dick Olsher reviewed the Gale GS402 loudspeaker in *Stereophile*, first published on 1 August 1985. [Stereophile: Gale GS402 loudspeaker](https://www.stereophile.com/content/gale-gs402-loudspeaker).

Olsher wrote:

> "As I understand it, the basic design resulted from a collaboration of Ira Gale and Sao Win, who were college classmates at the time."

What the review does and does not show:

- It is a review of the GS402 loudspeaker, a product of the D. W. Labs era. It is not about the GT2101 turntable.
- Olsher qualifies the statement with "As I understand it". That suggests information passed to him, not something he had established himself.
- The review gives no source for the statement.
- It is the earliest written source found so far for the story that Ira Gale and Sao Win were college classmates and collaborators.

Where Olsher got this information is not known. Which "basic design" the sentence refers to should be checked against the full text of the review: the quoted sentence on its own does not name the GT2101.

### A GT2101 owned by Sao Win

A current owner of a GT2101 has told the archive that he bought the turntable directly from Dr Sao Win. This is reported provenance and is recorded as genuine evidence of a physical connection between Sao Win and a GT2101.

An archived 2014 GaleAudio.com comment by "Robyatt" (identified in the GaleAudio.com notes as Robin Wyatt of Robyatt Audio) says: "I own Sao Win's personal turntable and have the emails from him to prove it." The archive has not confirmed whether this is the same machine or the same owner.

What is not yet known about this machine:

- when Sao Win acquired it
- whether he bought it new or second-hand
- whether he had any relationship with Gale when he acquired it
- whether he used it professionally
- whether he modified it
- whether it was ever fitted with a Win Labs tonearm
- whether it was connected with the development of any Win Labs product
- whether Dick Olsher ever saw it

The provenance is genuine evidence. Its significance is unresolved.

### Sao Win's own turntable work

Contemporary material records Sao Win's own audio work under the Win Laboratories (Win Labs) name:

- the SDT-10 strain-gauge cartridge, from about 1976;
- a 1979 *Audio* account of a Win turntable ([*Audio*, June 1979](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Audio/Archive-Audio/70s/Audio-1979-06-b.pdf)), which needs re-checking against the scan for its model designation;
- by about 1984, *Audio* describing a Win Labs SEC-10 turntable with a three-point suspended acrylic structure, a brushless DC motor, a magnetic motor bearing, a quartz reference, an infrared optical encoder and a digital servo. *(Exact issue still to be cited.)*

An [Audionics document](https://w140.com/tekwiki/images/e/e0/Audionics_Info.pdf) also acknowledges his assistance.

On this evidence, Sao Win's own turntable work appears to come later than the original GT2101 development. The SEC-10's features are similar to the GT2101's, but similarity does not show that one was derived from the other.

## 2. Later claims

These statements appear in later sources. None is supported by the patent, and none should be treated as established. Several of them may derive from a single earlier statement rather than being independent of one another.

| Claim | Where it appears | What the evidence says |
|---|---|---|
| Sao Win collaborated with Ira Gale on the design | *Stereophile*, 1985 ("As I understand it"); later GaleAudio.com text; Fremer, 2012 | Not named on the 1974 patent; Hobden has never heard of him in connection with Gale. Unsourced in every version found. |
| Gale and Win were college classmates | *Stereophile*, 1985; GaleAudio.com (Harvey Mudd College); Fremer, 2012 (college in California) | Not yet documented. No college record has been found. |
| Win helped co-develop the GT2101's DC servo system | GaleAudio.com | Contradicted by Ramsden's and Hobden's accounts of the electronics being done at DCA. Unsourced. |
| Gale and Win "shared the patents on the design" | Michael Fremer, [AnalogPlanet, 2012](https://www.analogplanet.com/content/legendary-gale-gt-2101-appears-my-door); GaleAudio.com ("They jointly held patents") | Contradicted by the 1974 patent, which does not name Win. No patent naming Win in connection with the GT2101 has been found. |

The GaleAudio.com text, as preserved, reads:

> "Collaborators: Ira Gale and Dr. Sao Win, classmates from Harvey Mudd College. Gale studied music at the Royal Academy of Music. Win studied at Cambridge and helped co-develop the DC servo system. They jointly held patents for the design."

See [Interviews & Provenance](/GT2101/interviews-provenance/#design--production-credits).

### How the story may have spread

A working reading of the sources, not an established chain:

1. 1985: Olsher's *Stereophile* review is the earliest surviving written version found so far, qualified as "As I understand it".
2. 2011–2016: GaleAudio.com repeats the Sao Win connection and expands it, adding Harvey Mudd College, Cambridge, the DC servo and joint patents.
3. 2012: Michael Fremer repeats the story on AnalogPlanet with the stronger claim that the two "shared the patents on the design".

The point for readers of this archive: a claim that appears on several modern websites does not necessarily have several independent sources. Several later accounts may come from the same original statement. Until an independent source is found, the repetition adds no weight.

## 3. Unresolved hypotheses

Each of the following is a possibility under investigation. None is supported by documentary evidence, and none should be quoted as an explanation.

### A. Wong and Win

By the early and mid-1980s Gale was owned by Donald Wong, trading as D. W. Labs. The 1985 review was of the GS402, a D. W. Labs-era product.

The names are similar: *Donald Wong / DW Labs* and *Sao Win / Win Labs*. One possible explanation under investigation is that the later D. W. Labs and Donald Wong history became conflated with Sao Win and Win Labs, perhaps while Olsher was giving the GS402 a history connecting it to the original Gale company. No documentary evidence has yet been found showing that this is what happened.

### B. A GT2101 influencing Win's own turntable

If Sao Win owned a GT2101 before developing his own turntable, that would give a plausible route by which the GT2101 could have influenced the Win Labs SEC-10. This is a hypothesis only. When he acquired the machine is unknown, and the similarities between the two decks do not by themselves show influence.

### C. Olsher encountering a GT2101 associated with Sao Win

It is conceivable that Olsher came across a GT2101 linked with Sao Win, and that this is how Win became associated with Gale in his account. There is no evidence that Olsher owned or saw a GT2101, or that he saw the machine later owned by Sao Win.

### D. A GT2101 fitted with a Win Labs tonearm

It is conceivable that a GT2101 was fitted with a Win Labs tonearm, and that seeing such a combination contributed to the association. There is no evidence that a Win Labs arm was ever fitted to a GT2101, to the machine Sao Win owned, or to any machine Olsher saw, or that Olsher's statement came from such an encounter.

### E. How Sao Win came to own a GT2101

Whether he bought it new or used, from Gale, D. W. Labs, a dealer or a private owner, and in what year, is unknown. The answer could support or weaken several of the hypotheses above.

## What would help

New primary evidence on any of these points could move the question forward:

- the full text of Olsher's 1985 review, and any *Stereophile* correspondence or notes on where the Sao Win statement came from;
- dated evidence of when and from whom Sao Win acquired his GT2101, such as the emails mentioned by its owner, a receipt or a serial number;
- photographs of that machine, especially its arm, arm board and any modifications;
- D. W. Labs press or dealer material for the GS402, to see whether it mentions Sao Win;
- college records showing whether Ira Gale and Sao Win studied at the same institution, and when;
- the original *Audio* scans for the Win Labs SDT-10 and SEC-10, with exact issue references;
- any patent naming Sao Win, to set against Fremer's claim.

If you can help with any of these, please get in touch through the archive's [GitHub issues](https://github.com/Gale-Electronics/gale-electronics.github.io/issues).
