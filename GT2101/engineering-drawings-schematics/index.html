A living archive of the GT2101 motor stack: reverse-engineered schematics, board layouts, high-res photos and bench notes. Some of this material was inherited from the defunct galeaudio.com and its original sources are unknown, so every claim on this page carries a status marker — ✅ confirmed against hardware, 📄 from a document, ❓ unverified. Nothing is deleted for being unverified; it is labelled.

The five boards — what each one actually does

The control tower holds five circular boards, numbered 1 at the top to 5 at the bottom, joined by a flexible ribbon backplane. Every claim below carries a status marker, so an inherited guess can never be mistaken for a measured fact:

✅ confirmed against the hardware — read off the part, or measured 📄 from a document — usually a hand-traced reverse-engineering sheet ❓ unverified — inherited from the defunct galeaudio.com material

BoardGale numberWhat it doesStatus
1 (top) GT201/3155ST ✅ Display. A three-digit frequency counter — an MC14553 counts the F DISPLAY line for a gated window and an MC14511 drives the LED digits. The decimal point is hardwired, so it can only ever read NN.N. 1332 Hz displays "33.3". ✅ number read off the board.

Correction — the "Disk 3 · Optical Sensor" description is wrong.
The inherited page Disk-3-Optical-Sensor.pdf, and the folder name that still matches it, describe board 3 as an optical sensor and tachometer processor "mounted close to the motor's internal photodiode assembly". Photographs of three spare boards and the four traced schematic sheets show nothing of the kind: there is no photodiode, no encoder input, and no such signal on the nine-pin connector. The XR2207 at the heart of the board is a voltage-controlled oscillator — it turns a voltage into a frequency, which is the opposite of what a tachometer processor does. That page should be read as ❓ disputed until someone can show where its claims came from. The folder name is left unchanged for now so that existing links keep working.
Board 5's part number is the one entry never read off the copper.
The toroidal mains transformer is bolted directly to board 5, covering the area of copper where the legend would be — which is why the photograph of its underside shows the transformer rather than an etched number. So 3285NH remains ❓ inherited. Worth knowing alongside it: the separate round motor PCB is etched GT2101 3185NH/B ✅, one digit away. Reading board 5's own number off the copper, while the board is out and the transformer unbolted, is the one measurement that would complete this register.

These boards were revised — check the issue letter

Gale did not freeze this design. Two of the five boards photographed here carry an issue marking etched beside the part number, and one of them exists in the archive in two different revisions with different components. ✅ All issue letters below were read directly off the boards.

  • GT201/3275ST — board 3, plain (no issue letter)
  • GT201/3275ST ISSUE B — board 3, a genuinely different circuit
  • GT201/3276ST ISSUE C — board 4
  • GT2101 3185NH/B — the separate motor PCB, not a tower board

Anyone comparing a board against these drawings should check the issue letter first.

Board 3 exists in two issues

Boards photographed for this archive carry two different revisions of the same part number. The plain GT201/3275ST is built around an LM3900 quad Norton amplifier; GT201/3275ST ISSUE B has visibly different copper routing and an NE555 in its place, with no LM3900 at all. ✅ Both legends were read directly off the boards.

The traced schematic sheets show the LM3900, so they describe the plain issue only. Anything measured on an ISSUE B board should be treated as a separate case until someone traces it. Component date codes on the plain boards run from 1974 to 1975.

Motor drive voltage — both sides of the gate

📄 The board 3 layout sheet tabulates the drive voltage at both ends of the gate. The backplane sheet only ever recorded one column — the input — which is where the widely repeated figure of "10 V at standstill" comes from.

ConditionBoard 3 pin 6 — demand inBoard 3 pin 7 — drive out
Still10 V0 V
33⅓ rpm1.2 V1.2 V
45 rpm1.6 V1.6 V
78 rpm2.4 V2.4 V

Running, the voltage passes straight through. Stopped, the demand rails to 10 V and board 3 holds its output at zero — the comparator sees still, the JFET mutes the drive, and the motor receives nothing. Anyone rebuilding this servo should note that the 10 V figure never reaches the motor, and that the idle drive voltage is 0 V. These figures are 📄 only, from a sheet marked preliminary; measuring pin 7 on a running deck would confirm them.
